### Hydronic compression index (CI)

Let’s define a simple, operator‑grade Compression Index that matches your pseudocode style.

We treat a basin/system as a sequence of pressure events with normalized weights \(p_i\) (like your κ, ρ, β, τ, μ, γ, σ parameters over time).

**Step 1: Effective skew (pressure inequality)**  
For a given time window, compute a normalized probability vector over dominant patterns/events:
\[
p_i = \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j}
\]
where \(w_i\) are observed frequencies/intensities (e.g., enforcement actions, evasive restructurings, backlog spikes).

Define **entropy**:
\[
H = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i
\]

Define **max entropy** for \(n\) patterns:
\[
H_{\max} = \log n
\]

Define **compression** as how far we are from max entropy:
\[
CI = 1 - \frac{H}{H_{\max}}
\]

- \(CI \approx 0\): flat distribution, high entropy, low curvature → **entropy‑friendly, resilient**.  
- \(CI \approx 1\): highly skewed distribution, low entropy, high curvature → **over‑compressed, brittle, collapse‑prone**.

You can compute CI per basin (wound, institution, regulatory regime) and track it over time like any of your κ, ρ, β, τ, μ, γ, σ parameters.

#### 2. Compression Index (CI)

- **Concept:**  
  CI measures how much a basin/system has compressed its pressure history into a single brittle attractor.

- **Formula:**  
  - Let \(p_i\) be normalized weights of dominant patterns/events.  
  - Entropy: \(H = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i\).  
  - Max entropy: \(H_{\max} = \log n\).  
  - Compression Index:
    \[
    CI = 1 - \frac{H}{H_{\max}}
    \]

- **Interpretation:**  
  - Low CI → high entropy, low curvature, many viable paths → resilient, entropy‑friendly.  
  - High CI → low entropy, high curvature, few viable paths → brittle, collapse‑prone.

- **Applications:**  
  - **Wound basins:** CI tracks how repeated relational patterns compress identity/expectation into trauma attractors.  
  - **Institutions:** CI tracks how repeated self‑protective decisions compress legitimacy into a narrow, brittle code.  
  - **Regulatory regimes (China outbound model):** CI tracks how enforcement patterns and branching behaviors compress the system into lock‑in vs. compliant basins.
